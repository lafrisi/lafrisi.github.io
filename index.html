01.1 / THE MECHANISM

Start with
the material.

the challenge

Salt can bridge inside a hopper instead of feeding into the mechanism. The printed auger was designed to reduce bridging and support consistent volumetric dispensing.

I connected the hopper and auger assembly to a DC/servo motor drive—the first layer of a complete mechatronic system.

01.2 / THE CONNECTION

Beyond the
local network.

system integration

The ESP32 and Python relay connect the physical device to remote monitoring. Tailscale provides VPN connectivity across NAT boundaries so the device can be reached from another network.

The challenge was bringing the mechanism, electronics, network, and interface into one coherent system.

ESP32→Python relay→Remote UI
01.3 / THE INTERFACE

Give the interface
real context.

human + hardware

Live device telemetry is passed into a conversational interface, combining Gemini Pro, ElevenLabs text-to-speech, and a Tkinter UI. The conversation can refer to what the device is actually doing.

My next validation focus: mass delivered per command, repeatability, and recovery from a jam or a lost connection.

Peel a bonded specimen in a controlled, repeatable way. I developed a fixture around a hollow drum, a supported stage, and a linear actuator—then explored how to turn its motion into useful force measurements.

the mechanical logic

Motion with a purpose.

The linear actuator controls the stage’s feed. A catch on the drum engages the specimen as it peels. The end-cap wheels fit the square aluminum rod into the hollow drum, connecting it to a pair of aluminum leaf springs supported by wooden blocks.

the measurement concept

From deflection to force.

The sensing concept maps sheet-metal deflection to force. After taring and calibration, the relationship is simple; making the measurement reliable takes more work.

F = kδk = 15.5 N/mm
MODEL VALUE

Alignment, compliance, repeatability, and the elastic operating range determine how useful the force estimate will be.

03.1 / ACQUIRE

Better inputs.
Better evidence.

the experiment

I studied how glue-streak geometry changes over curing time, supporting a broader investigation of adhesive behavior and cold noise in the ATLAS Inner Tracker.

A copper-tape scale supported image-to-distance calibration. Consistent imaging and pilot glue deposits helped make comparisons more meaningful.

03.2 / EXTRACT

Turn pixels
into geometry.

my contribution

I built an OpenCV workflow for streak segmentation and contour-based measurements. It turns deposit boundaries into quantities that can be tracked across batches and curing times.

03.3 / COMPARE

Follow the
distribution.

interpreting the result

Violin plots show the spread of streak lengths at each time point. Time-series analysis and ANOVA supported comparisons across the study.

These are measurements of geometry. Connecting them to adhesive strength requires mechanical testing—the reason image analysis and fixture design belong in the same conversation.

Good analysis depends as much on the experiment as it does on the code.
